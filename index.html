01 · Briefing

TOPIC AND RESEARCH QUESTION

MOTIVATION FOR THE PROJECT TOPIC

Why this intrigued me

I use screens every single day, but I never really stopped to think what was happening on the inside. From far away a monitor such as my own computers looks like one smooth image, but once you zoom in you realise millions of tiny pixels arranged in accurate geometric patterns are what form our technology screens. In this project I wanted to understand and educate my peers on how shapes, grids, light and unusual materials work together to create the photos and videos we see. This project goes on to show that geometry and chemistry aren't just things we learn in class but expand and work as one in the little things we use to drive our lives and stare at all day.

WHY THE QUESTION HOLDS

A strong research question

This topic connects two subjects math, through grids, coordinates and ratios, and science through materials, light and chemistry allowing for a perfect final project for ISM10. It is also researchable because the physics and chemistry behind screens are very well documented. Screens are a huge advancement for billions of people every day which makes the topic relevant. This also allows for comparison because geometry chemistry, light and materials could each be studied and tested side by side.

MY RESEARCH QUESTION
“How are geometry and chemistry able to work together in order to create the images we see on technology screens?”

STAGE 01 / 06

Inside the screen

A sealed monitor panel forms together in layers, which consists of an aluminum front bezel, glowing liquid glass in the middle, and a matching back bezel. Geometry defines the structure of it, whilst chemistry creates the image you see through the manipulation of light.

STAGE 02 / 06

Front Bezel Lifts

The front frame lifts away and flies upward, which reveals the glowing core beneath it (LCD, liquid crystal display). This layer is full geometry a rectangular border that keeps the panel in a flat state, and aligned with microscopic accuracy.

STAGE 03 / 06

The Core

The center is a thick liquid crystal glass sheet, glowing with cyan and amber light inside, sealed inside is the blacklight and the color filter which is the display's chemistry into one bright pane.

STAGE 04 / 06

Inside the Core

The particle field represents the liquid crystal itself, which consists of rod shaped molecules that are twisted into 90° helix that guide light. As soon as voltage is applied the molecules stand upright as the twist breaks apart and the pixel gets darker, so from brightness value 0 to 255 each one is basically controlled by a different voltage level.

STAGE 05 / 06

Pixel Shapes

The camera moves closer to the screen until the image is separated into individual rectangular cells. Geometry is responsible for every pixel its shape, spacing, and its position while the glowing material underneath control how bright each cell becomes thanks to chemistry.

STAGE 06 / 06

Back Bezel

The back frame falls apart and than all the layers snap into place, geometry positions every part on the grid while chemistry controls the colors and light. Multiply that by 2,073,600 pixels refreshing 60 times every second (60hz) and a full image appears on our technology screens.

03 · Molecular Exhibit

Liquid Crystal

PHASE 1 / 3 — DATA and ANALYSIS

From Signal to Image

Every single set frame the display controller sends voltage instructions to each point on the screens grid. In this 3d view each indivisual particle represents one piece of data, which floats without order until the screens material turn it into visible light + color.

PHASE 2 / 3 — Order + Shape

Liquid Crystals Take Shape

The points that were dissassembled and scattered around the screen form into rod shaped liquid crystal molecules, which is a strange state of matter that moves like a liquid but lines up as if it were crystal. Trapped between both glass plates the molecules naturally align themselves into a spiral shape, with each layer rotating a little more than the one below it until it twists a full 90 degrees from bottom to top.

PHASE 3 / 3 — FIELD APPLIED

Voltage breaks said twist

An electric field pulls in the liquid crystal rods upright, which breaks the spiral that was guiding light through the pixel. When that happens the pixel's light valve shuts. The molecules relax back into their twisted shape. For example on a gaming monitor this switching can happen up to 240 times per second. (known as a 240hz display)

04 · The Pixel Timeline

A Century of tiny pixels

1897
1897

Cathode ray tube

Karl Ferdinand Braun a German physcist inventor builds the first CRT which is essentially an electron beam making a phosphor screen, every electronic display descends from this, including modern ones.

1954
1954

Color lands

In 1954 the RCA's CT 100 becomes one of the first color TVs used tiny groups of red, green and blue (RGB) to create phosphor dots which were used to create color, this is an early version of subpixels that are used today.

1965
1965

“Pixel” gets its name

An American engineer named Frederic Billingsley who worked at NASA's jet labratory published the word we now know and use "pixel" when he used it describe the small picture elements that made up images in space probes.

1968
1968

The first LCD

The first LCD (liquid crystal display) was showed off by George Heilmeir and his team at RCA, this essentially turned liquid crystals which is a strange state of matter studied in chemistry into something that could be used as a screen.

1973
1973

LCD goes pocket size

One of the first handheld uses of a LCD screen was Sharp's EL 805 calculator which brought liquid crystals to consumers, and ran on a tiny battery for months at a time since the valve only blocks light.

1987
1987

VGA 640 × 480 (480p)

In 1987 IBM's VGA set a new line for computer screens with 307,200 pixels and from that point on resolution became a number the average computer user recognized.

2007
2007

The OLED era starts

Sony releases a new ground breaking invention, the Oled display, no longer relying on the previous liquid crystal but rather a grid of organic light emitting diodes, short for OLED.

2010
2010

Naked eyes limits

Apples 326 PPI known as Retina, displays pack pixels smaller than the naked eye can resolve

8K
TODAY

Millions of Pixels at Once

Current modern 8k screens contain 7680 x 4320 pixels adding up to 33,177,600 tiny points of light, and as the geometry becomes more advanced the chemistry follows behind to control the light and color.

HOW DO THEY COINCIDE

Geometry is responsible for organizing pixels into a very accurate grid, and decides where each color belongs and appears, while chemistry controls the light inside each pixel and helps decide what appears on that same screen. Without geometry the pixels would be scattered without a clear image and without chemistry there would be a grid with nothing to show, nothing to spark the light into the images we see on a daily basis.
